I get asked some version of "what should we be doing with AI?" in almost every first call. The honest answer is less exciting than LinkedIn would have you believe, and a lot more useful.

Here's my scorecard, based on what I've built and seen running in real teams, not on demos.

## Working well

**Reading at scale.** This is the most underrated use. Survey answers, reviews, call transcripts, support tickets, social comments. Models are very good at reading thousands of messy texts and telling you what's in them. Teams used to sample ten percent and guess at the rest. Now they can read all of it.

**First drafts and variants.** Ad copy variations, email versions, product descriptions, social adaptations. With good examples and a human editor, this saves real hours without anyone noticing a drop in quality.

**Triage and routing.** Sorting inbound leads, requests and emails into the right queue with a short reason. It's dull, it's valuable, and it's where agent-style workflows earn their keep first.

**Enrichment and research.** Pulling together what you need to know about a company or a contact before a call. Tools like Clay paired with an LLM step have made this dramatically faster.

**Analysis help.** Asking questions of your campaign data in plain English, spotting anomalies, writing the first version of a weekly performance summary. You still check the numbers. You just start further along.

## Promising, but handle with care

**Personalization at scale.** It works when you have good data and a small number of meaningful segments. It goes badly when it means generating a slightly different email for every contact based on thin data. People notice, and it's often creepier than it is helpful.

**AI-generated visuals.** Great for concepts, mood boards and quick social assets. Risky for hero brand work, both for quality and for the questions it raises about rights and authenticity.

**Search and AI answers.** More of your audience is asking chatbots instead of search engines. Clear, well-structured, genuinely useful content on your own site is still the best way to show up. There's no secret trick here yet, whatever the new acronyms promise.

## Mostly a demo

**Fully autonomous campaigns.** The idea that you can give an AI a budget and a goal and walk away. I haven't seen it work outside of narrow, well-instrumented paid media tests, and even those need a person watching.

**Replacing strategy.** Models can summarize your market. They can't decide what your brand should stand for or which bet to make this year. That's still the job.

## Where I'd spend the first hour

If you're just starting, don't buy a platform. Find the most boring, repetitive reading task on your team. Something a smart person spends hours on every week and secretly hates. Try automating just that one thing, measure the hours before and after, and keep a human checking the output for the first month.

That project will teach your team more about AI than any training session, and it will probably pay for the next three.
